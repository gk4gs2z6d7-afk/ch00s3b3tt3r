The Harder Road

Why Rage Is Not Enough — And Exactly What to Do Instead
Kai Jashon Price · Structural Humanism
↓ Word (.docx) Public Record · Distribute Freely

THE ARGUMENT IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The documented failures in this series — manufactured division, financial architecture designed for extraction, foreign policy blowback, suppressed accountability, captured healthcare — did not happen by accident. They were built by identifiable people using identifiable mechanisms, and they are sustained by the absence of organized, persistent, legal accountability. The appropriate response is not rage, not despair, and not violence. It is organized, legal, relentless civic pressure applied through every constitutional mechanism available until the cost of maintaining these systems exceeds the cost of changing them. This is harder than rage. It is also the only thing that works.

SOURCE: Chenoweth, E. & Stephan, M. (2011). Why Civil Resistance Works. Columbia University Press. — Analysis of 323 resistance campaigns found nonviolent campaigns succeeded at twice the rate of violent ones: 53% versus 26%.

WHY VIOLENCE FAILS STRATEGICALLY

Non-violence is not chosen out of weakness or sentimentality. It is chosen because the strategic record shows it works better. Erica Chenoweth and Maria Stephan's study of 323 resistance campaigns between 1900 and 2006 found that nonviolent campaigns succeeded approximately twice as often as violent ones. The mechanism is not moral — it is structural. Violent resistance alienates the broad coalition that successful reform requires. It provides justification for the repression it seeks to overcome. It transfers moral authority from reformers to the system being reformed. And it reliably produces new versions of the concentrated power it was supposed to eliminate.

The wars in Vietnam and Iraq illustrate the pattern from the state's perspective: violent interventionist campaigns imposed enormous financial and human costs, turned service members and civilians against each other in mutual blame, insulated the officials who drove the policies from accountability, and produced the blowback documented in this series. Violence is the path of failure regardless of which side of it you're on.

WHERE PERSONAL RESPONSIBILITY BEGINS

The framework of judging systems rather than people requires a precise answer to where individual accountability begins — because without that answer, "systems, not people" becomes a permanent excuse for individuals who benefit from unjust systems.

Aristotle distinguished in Nicomachean Ethics between involuntary ignorance — for which a person bears no fault — and culpable ignorance: the deliberate choice not to know what is knowable, for which they do bear fault. The threshold is the moment of undeniable confrontation with documented evidence. Before that confrontation, the system that managed the ignorance bears primary responsibility. After it, the person who chooses comfortable avoidance bears responsibility for that choice.

This document is that confrontation. Every person who reads it and returns to comfortable deference to institutional authority has made a choice, not suffered a condition. The moral burden shifts at the moment of knowing.

SOURCE: Aristotle. Nicomachean Ethics, Book III, 1110b-1111b.

THE CONSTITUTIONAL FOUNDATION

The Declaration of Independence states explicitly that when government becomes destructive of its ends — the protection of life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness — it is the right and the duty of the people to alter or abolish it. This is not radical doctrine. It is the foundational document of the American republic, containing an explicit theory of legitimate resistance.

Every member of the United States military swears an oath to support and defend the Constitution against all enemies, foreign and domestic — not to any president, not to any party, not to any commanding officer. The Nuremberg Principles, established in international law after World War II, hold that following orders does not excuse participation in crimes. UCMJ Article 92 requires military personnel to refuse unlawful orders. The oath is to the document. The obligation is to the document. The people in uniform who understand this are the most important constituency for constitutional reform in America.

THE SPECIFIC ACTION FRAMEWORK — START TODAY

Do these in order. Each one takes less than an hour.

ONE: Go to opensecrets.org. Look up your federal representative's voting record on pharmaceutical price negotiation and their pharmaceutical industry campaign contributions. Both are public. The correlation requires no interpretation. Share what you find with five people.

TWO: Go to foia.gov. File one Freedom of Information Act request. Request documents related to the 1962-63 Senate Foreign Relations Committee investigation of the American Zionist Council, or the 1967 USS Liberty Court of Inquiry. What remains classified tells you something. What is released tells you something. Either way, you have used the legal mechanism that exists for exactly this purpose.

THREE: Call — not email, call — your congressional representative's office. Ask one specific question: does the representative support conditioning Department of Defense appropriations on completion of an independent financial audit? Record the response. Share it.

FOUR: Attend one local government meeting — city council, school board, county commission — within the next thirty days. The people making decisions that most directly affect your daily life meet in public. Most seats are empty. Your presence changes the cost calculation of the people in front of the room.

FIVE: Identify one person in your life whose political conclusions differ from yours. Have one conversation starting from a documented fact in this series rather than from tribal identity. Not a debate. A conversation. The solidarity available on September 12th is always available. The only thing preventing it is the system's successful management of our attention.

THE RIVER

The distance between an informed person and a civic actor is almost always one specific action that feels smaller than the problem. The problem is large. The action does not need to match its scale. It needs to start the compound interest of organized pressure — which accumulates the same way debt does, but in the other direction.

Every movement that changed America started with people doing things that felt too small for the scale of what they were trying to change. They did them anyway. The river does not ask permission. It simply flows. You do not need to be perfect. You only need to refuse to stay silent.

Choose Better — not someday, but now.
